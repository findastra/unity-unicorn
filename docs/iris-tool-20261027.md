# iris-tool

Astra, 2026-10-27: "Lets make this a toggable thing, is it RGB able too? Can we call this iris-tool so we can use it on other avis?"

iris-tool puts an iris pack (like *Look Into the Stars*) on an avatar's eyes behind a menu toggle. The avatar keeps its pupils, its eye tracking and its RGB, and its own eye materials are never changed: the toggle swaps the eye slots to star copies.

Open it in Unity from **Tools › Unity Unicorn › iris-tool** after copying `scripts/unity-editor/iris-tool-20261027.cs` into `Assets/UnityUnicorn/Editor/`. It needs VRCFury for the toggle.

## Two kinds of eyes

Avatars draw irises in two different ways, and the tool handles both.

| | Texture eyes | Love Eye eyes |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Poppy (Poiyomi, Eden's Serpent eye texture) | Obsi (Iamres' Love Eye Shader, locked) |
| What draws the iris | a picture: one iris filling the eye texture | the shader, live: noise, glow, pupil shape, parallax |
| What the tool does | cuts the iris out of the pack and stretches it onto the eye texture's iris oval | copies the avatar's own locked shader and adds a picture layer under its pupil |
| Pupils | the pack's dark centre replaces a pupil painted in the old texture; pupil blendshapes still move the mesh | the shader's pupil (heart, star, cat ...) and its glow stay on top |
| Eye tracking | eye bones and blendshapes are untouched | the picture follows the shader's parallax uv, so the iris still tracks the camera |
| RGB | the copy keeps the material's hue settings and animated property names | the picture turns with the material's own hue offset and hue cycling |

## Steps

1. Pick the **Avatar** (the object with the avatar descriptor) and the **Mesh with the eyes**. The window ticks the slots that look like irises (Love Eye materials, and materials named "eye" that are not eyeballs, lashes or brows); change the ticks if needed.
2. Pick the **Iris pack picture**, and its emission map if it has one.
3. **Cut from**: where the pack's one iris sits, in the picture file's own pixels with the top-left corner as 0,0 (the numbers an image editor shows). Pack sheets made for another avatar hold several parts; take one iris.
4. **Place at**: for texture eyes, the iris oval on the avatar's eye texture (open it in an image editor and read the oval's box). For Love Eye eyes, the whole square (0, 0, size, size): the shader fits the square to its iris.
5. **Menu toggle**, for example `Eyes/Star Irises`, and **Save in** (a folder under Assets).
6. **Make star copies and the toggle**. The scene is saved; the toggle is off by default and remembered.

New files are named `<name>-YYYYMMDD.<ext>`; running the tool again reuses them, so a shader is never defined twice.

## What it was first used on (2026-10-27)

- **Obsi** (Love Eye): cut 62, 185, 880 × 880 from Selestia's 4096 *Look Into the Stars* sheet, placed at 0, 0, 1024 × 1024. Her heart pupil and glow stayed; a hue offset of 0.33 turned the stars green, 0 left them purple.
- **Poppy** (texture eye), done by hand with the same steps just before the tool existed: cut 25, 47, 882 × 1130 from the same sheet, placed at 243, 74, 1580 × 1728 on her 2048 Serpent eye texture.

## Things to know

- Love Eye's own HSV round trip (`RGBToHSVtoRGB3`) is not neutral at a hue of 0 and turned purple stars green, so the iris layer uses a plain hue rotation.
- Unity's `CopyPropertiesFromMaterial` leaves a material unable to keep values for properties the source material lacks, so the tool copies values one by one.
- Love Eye is a paid shader: the tool patches the copy in the user's project and never ships its code. Keep generated Love Eye Stars shaders and paid iris packs out of public repos.
- Not tested in VRChat yet.
